Privacy Policy

CubeLab is a free, offline-first speedcubing timer. This policy explains what data the app handles, where it is stored, when it can leave your device, and the choices you have.

Last updated: November 23, 2026 Applies to the CubeLab website and installed PWA

Local by default

Solves, sessions, settings, and Training history are stored in your browser on your device.

No CubeLab account

CubeLab does not operate a user account system, advertising service, or separate analytics or telemetry service.

Google Drive is optional

If you connect Google Drive, CubeLab can back up and synchronize through your own Google account using the hidden app-data folder.

Hosting is separate

Vercel delivers the site and may process standard request and security data under its own policies.

1. Overview

CubeLab is operated by Joshua Wang, an individual based in Toronto, Ontario, Canada. In this policy, “CubeLab,” “we,” “us,” and “our” refer to that project and operator. “You” means the person using CubeLab.

This policy applies to the CubeLab web application, its installable Progressive Web App (PWA), and these legal pages. It does not apply to third-party websites or services, including Google Drive, Google sign-in, or Vercel hosting. Those services have their own privacy policies.

CubeLab is designed to work without an account. You can use the timer, sessions, statistics, Analytics, Training, imports, and local backups without connecting Google Drive or sending your solve data to CubeLab.

2. Data CubeLab stores

CubeLab processes the information you create or import while using the app. Depending on the features you use, this can include:

  • Timer results, including raw times, +2 or DNF penalties, effective times, inspection state, dates, and solve identifiers.
  • Scrambles, session names, comments, competition round membership, event selections, and per-session preferences.
  • Statistics and Analytics data calculated from your solve history, such as averages, personal bests, trends, distributions, and activity summaries.
  • Training history, including OLL/PLL attempts, recognition results, case selections, calibration snapshots, practice visits, timing statistics, and Training preferences.
  • App settings, theme and appearance choices, tool-panel layout, onboarding state, and local interface preferences.
  • Imported csTimer data, including session names, solve records, scramble metadata, and import provenance.
  • Local synchronization metadata if you use Google Drive, such as a random device identifier, field timestamps, deletion records, conflict decisions, snapshot retention settings, and the last remote file identity. OAuth access tokens are not included because they are kept in memory only.
  • Local recovery and safety records used for Undo, replacement recovery, storage upgrades, and reset coordination.

CubeLab does not ask for your name, postal address, phone number, or date of birth. It does not create a CubeLab account, and it does not receive your Google account email or profile through the Drive integration.

CubeLab uses this information only to provide the features you request: timing, solve history, statistics, Analytics, training, imports, local backups, optional Google Drive synchronization, and optional Online Battle. It is not used for advertising, profiling, credit decisions, or sale to third parties.

3. Local storage, processing, and backups

Your CubeLab data is processed in your browser. It is stored using browser technologies such as localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, and service-worker caches. This storage belongs to the CubeLab origin in your browser profile.

By default, CubeLab does not transmit your application data to a CubeLab server. CubeLab has no application backend, database, user account, advertising network, or telemetry pipeline of its own. Scrambles are generated locally by the bundled open-source cubing.js library. Fonts and app assets are served by the site or cached locally.

Local exports and imports

You can export your data as a native CubeLab backup (JSON, or checked gzip for larger backups) and import it later. You can also import compatible csTimer data. These files are created and read by your browser at your request. They are not uploaded to CubeLab.

Backup files may contain your full solve and Training history, comments, and settings. They are not encrypted by CubeLab; gzip only reduces file size. Store them somewhere you trust and delete copies you no longer need.

The CubeLab PWA uses a service worker to cache the application shell and assets so the app can work offline. Those caches are stored on your device and can be removed with your browser’s site-data controls or CubeLab’s Reset All Site Data action.

4. Google Drive backup and synchronization

Google Drive backup and synchronization are optional. CubeLab does not connect to Google Drive unless you choose Connect Google Drive and complete Google’s authorization flow.

What CubeLab accesses

CubeLab uses Google Identity Services and requests only this OAuth scope:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata

Google describes this scope as: “See, create, and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive.” It lets CubeLab list, download, upload, and delete files that CubeLab created in its own hidden app-data folder. It does not let CubeLab browse your other Drive files, Gmail, Photos, contacts, calendar, or Google profile.

CubeLab deliberately does not request email, profile, openid, or other identity scopes. The connected account label stored in your browser is a generic status (“Google Drive connected”), not your Google email address.

What is stored remotely
Solve snapshots contain sessions, solves (including times, penalties, scrambles, comments, dates, and competition fields), settings, and synchronization metadata. Training snapshots contain Training attempts, timing and recognition data, calibration snapshots, practice visits, preferences, and Training sync metadata.
File metadata
CubeLab-created files use names such as cubelab-backup-<device>-<random>.json and cubelab-training-<device>-<random>.json. CubeLab also stores app-private metadata such as the format, version, and a sanitized device identifier.
Purpose
To provide the backup and multi-device synchronization features you request. Google user data is not used for advertising, third-party analytics, profiling, creditworthiness, or sale.
Size limits
Solve snapshots are capped at 10 MB. Training snapshots are capped at 16 MB compact JSON. Oversized data is stopped before upload.
Where it is held
In your own Google Drive app-data folder under your Google account. CubeLab does not operate a server that receives or stores these snapshots. Your browser communicates with Google APIs directly over HTTPS.
Retention
Remote snapshots remain until you delete them or CubeLab’s retention cleanup removes superseded files. The default policy keeps the newest snapshot of every device and up to 10 snapshots per device for 30 days; either the count or age limit can trigger cleanup. The legacy shared file is not removed by automatic cleanup. Manual Training backup cleanup keeps the newest five of this device.

Google API Limited Use disclosure

CubeLab’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In plain language: CubeLab uses Google Drive app-data only to provide the backup and synchronization features you ask for. We do not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or transfer it to data brokers or advertisers. We do not read your Google data except as necessary to operate the feature you requested, secure the service, comply with law, or with your direction.

Access tokens

Google returns an OAuth access token after you authorize CubeLab. CubeLab keeps that token in memory for the current page session. It is not written to localStorage, IndexedDB, native backups, or Google Drive. CubeLab treats the token as valid for about 55 minutes, and it is cleared when you disconnect, reset site data, or reload or close the page. CubeLab does not store a refresh token. Local connection metadata (a generic connection label, the last file identity, and synchronization preferences) may remain in your browser.

Disconnecting and revoking access

  • In CubeLab, open Settings → Data → Google Drive backup and choose Disconnect. This ends the connection, attempts to revoke the current access token, and turns automatic synchronization off.
  • You can also revoke CubeLab’s access at any time from your Google Account permissions page. Revoking access does not automatically delete snapshots already stored in your Google Drive app-data folder.

Deleting remote backups

In Settings → Data → Google Drive backup, the Remote snapshots section lets you delete individual solve snapshots. Delete remote backup removes every CubeLab solve snapshot, including the legacy cubelab-backup.json file, but does not delete local data. Training snapshots are a separate file family and can be deleted individually in the Training Drive section. Automatic cleanup also removes superseded snapshots according to your retention settings.

Reset All Site Data does not delete Google Drive files. If you revoke or disconnect access before deleting remote files, you may need to reconnect to remove them through CubeLab.

5. Online Battle

Online Battle is optional. It uses a serverless WebRTC data channel between two browsers. You and the other participant manually exchange an invitation code and an answer code; CubeLab does not operate a signaling server, account, or battle relay.

During a battle, the two browsers exchange the event, scramble, race start and reset signals, and each participant’s result directly. The other participant’s browser and network may also receive technical connection information, such as IP addresses, as part of establishing the peer-to-peer connection. The room ID and password are access gates, not a guarantee that the other participant is trustworthy.

CubeLab uses Google’s public STUN server stun.l.google.com:19302 for NAT discovery. That server helps the browsers find a direct route; it does not relay your solve history. Google may process network metadata under its own privacy policy.

Battle results are shown for the race and are not added to your solve history by the Battle tool. Do not use Online Battle to share sensitive information, and only connect with people you trust.

6. Retention and deletion

CubeLab does not set an automatic expiry for local solve, settings, or Training data. It remains in your browser until you delete it, clear site data, reset CubeLab, or your browser removes it (for example because of storage pressure or a private-browsing session).

  • You can delete individual solves and comments, clear a session, reset all solve data, or delete sessions using CubeLab’s controls.
  • Reset All Site Data clears CubeLab’s local and session storage, CubeLab IndexedDB databases, CubeLab service-worker caches and registrations, and CubeLab-prefixed cookies. It does not delete unrelated browser data, browser HTTP caches managed outside the page, or Google’s own cookies.
  • Native backup files and downloaded copies remain wherever you saved them. Delete them with your file manager or cloud storage provider.
  • Google Drive snapshots are retained as described in the Google Drive section above. Deleting local data or resetting CubeLab does not delete remote snapshots.

Because CubeLab is local-first, we generally cannot access or delete data stored only in your browser. You control that data directly. If you contact us about a privacy request, we may ask for enough information to understand the request, but we cannot retrieve data that never reached us.

8. Cookies and browser storage

CubeLab does not set cookies of its own. It uses browser storage (localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, and service-worker caches) to keep your solves, settings, Training data, and app state on your device.

When you choose to connect Google Drive, Google Identity Services loads from Google and may use Google cookies or similar storage on Google domains. Google controls that storage under its own policies. Vercel may also use technically necessary cookies or similar storage for hosting, security, or delivery; see Vercel’s policy.

You can manage or clear browser storage through your browser’s site-data controls. Clearing CubeLab’s storage removes local app data and may sign you out of the optional Drive connection. It does not delete Google Drive snapshots.

9. Security and its limits

CubeLab is served over HTTPS. Google Drive requests use Google’s OAuth flow, the narrow app-data scope, and an access token held only in memory. Google protects Google Drive data under its own security terms.

CubeLab does not encrypt your local browser storage. Anyone with access to your device, browser profile, or an unencrypted backup file may be able to read your solve history and comments. Browser storage can also be lost through device failure, browser cleanup, storage pressure, private browsing, or accidental deletion. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, and CubeLab cannot guarantee absolute security.

Use a device lock, keep your browser and Google account secure, use a strong Google password and two-factor authentication, and keep independent backups of important data. Do not use CubeLab for highly sensitive information.

If you believe a child under 13 has used CubeLab or provided personal information, contact us so we can help you remove local data and address the situation. Google account age requirements also apply to the optional Drive connection.
